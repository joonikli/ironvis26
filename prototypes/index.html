Five ways to make CMU's AI habits visible

The same findings drawn five ways. Each one shows a single idea at a time. Compare them and pick what to keep; nothing here is final. All numbers come from the pipeline (dashboard/data.js), and only the local-good results are used.

Prototype A · Illustration

Six server rooms: Dev's and Sandbox's run bigger, read longer prompts, and blink red more often

Each group is drawn as a small server room. The racks show monthly energy, the paper pile shows how long a typical prompt is, and the status lights show broken requests.

Prints to PDF: yes
Strength: the gap between the groups is visible without reading a number
Weakness: the blocks are rounded to whole units, so it isn't precise
Prototype B · Unit chart

Take 100 OpenAI requests. The 10 that went to the reasoning model ran up 65 of every 100 dollars.

Each square is 1 of 100. Switch between requests, cost and energy and watch the same 100 squares change hands.

What one request costs, in coins

One coin = the average cost of one frontier request (about ).
Prints to PDF: yes, as three small panels side by side
Strength: "1 in 10 → 2 in 3" is easy to see and remember
Weakness: OpenAI groups only; one idea per picture
Prototype C · Interactive toy

Build a request, and see what your habits cost

Pick a model, then set how long the prompt is, how much of it is reused, and how long the answer is. The verdict compares your request with a typical request on the same model from CMU's efficient groups.

Prints to PDF: partly; it would print as 3 preset snapshots
Strength: teaches habits that work with any model; well suited to students
Weakness: it's a model of one request, not a picture of what campus actually did
Prototype D · Scrollytelling

The journey of one prompt

Scroll down. One page of text ≈ 500 tokens (about 375 words).

Prints to PDF: no; it would print as a comic strip of 4–6 frames
Strength: explains tokens, caching and hidden reasoning in plain language
Weakness: the most build time; a typical request, not totals
Prototype E · Everyday comparisons

A month of CMU's AI use takes about as much energy as charging a phone 20,000 times

Kilowatt-hours are abstract. Phone charges and lightbulb minutes aren't. Every comparison keeps its estimate range.

Prints to PDF: yes
Strength: instantly relatable, especially for a non-technical reader
Weakness: each comparison adds an assumption (phone battery, bulb wattage)
